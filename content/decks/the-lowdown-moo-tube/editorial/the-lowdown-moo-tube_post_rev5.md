# The Lowdown Moo Tube: Find the Horn, Blow the Horn, Bring the Beef

**Commander:** Sethron, Hurloon General (Black/Red)
**Original list:** [The Lowdown Moo Tube on Moxfield](https://moxfield.com/decks/y2FahPnUCUuTRHirl_Qn_g)
**Deck by:** Andrew ([Cute_Balls](https://moxfield.com/users/Cute_Balls) on Moxfield)
**This week's herd:** Budget Jank · Original Jank · Maximum Jank

---

## Why this deck exists

Some decks start with a combo. Some start with a spreadsheet. This one started with a noise, the low, wonderful, slightly ridiculous drone of a didgeridoo. Andrew tells it best, so here he is in his own words. We've only cleaned up spelling, capitalization and punctuation, and added paragraph breaks. Anything in [square brackets] is an editor's change; the bracketed line about Minotaurs was softened at Andrew's request.

> The Lowdown Moo Tube comes from my love of unique or funny-sounding words and game play mechanics. First off. Didgeridoo? Fantastic instrument in my opinion. Up there with Mongolian throat singing that produces [a sound] that just burrows in your ear. But with cards like the Didgeridoo or Dingus Staff, when I learn about them, I can't get them out of my head until I build something around it.
>
> I have also had a recent obsession with vintage cards and [Minotaurs have rarely gotten the spotlight since the early days of Magic, Sethron aside]. This gave me a unique opportunity to build two things simultaneously, a deck that gets to explore vintage cards and scratch that funky-name itch but not being held back by the constraints of vintage-only cards.
>
> And the name of the deck... A deck name in my opinion is nearly as important as the deck itself. I am tired of hearing about a bracket whatever mid-range spell-slinging deck or a proliferate deck... I want a named deck that tells you what it is in the most unique way possible. And the Lowdown Moo Tube is inspired by the jive names for other instruments like the "Licorice Stick".

That's the whole brief: a card with a name you can't stop saying, a creature type with a lot of history, and permission to use anything newer that keeps the joke going. This is a deck built to be fun and to sound like its owner. Winning is welcome, but the cows are the point.

## Meet the band

The original Moxfield description sums the deck up in four beats, and honestly we can't improve on it: **find the horn, blow the horn, make more beef, turn it sideways.**

- **The horn.** Didgeridoo is a one-mana artifact from Homelands (1995) that says: *"{3}: You may put a Minotaur permanent card from your hand onto the battlefield."* You blow the horn and a cow walks in. That's the whole instrument.
- **The bandleader.** Sethron, Hurloon General makes a 2/3 red Minotaur token whenever Sethron or *another nontoken* Minotaur you control enters. Every real cow brings a plus-one.
- **The big finish.** For {2}{B/R}, Sethron gives your Minotaurs +1/+0, menace and haste until end of turn. Add Shared Animosity or Rage Reflection and the herd charges.
- **The old-timers.** Every version keeps the Homelands Anaba crew (Anaba Ancestor and Anaba Spirit Crafter), and Budget and Maximum invite even more vintage cows.

Whatever the budget, two members never leave the band: **Sethron in the command zone and Didgeridoo in the deck**, plus at least one card that can go and fetch the horn.

## Original Jank: Andrew's version

This is the deck as it sits on Moxfield, card for card. We didn't touch it. Priced at Andrew's own printings, it comes to **$429.41 excluding basic lands** (Scryfall USD, 2026-10-29 19:20 JST; see the note on Blood Crypt and Adaptive Automaton above). It's the creator's version: old-school cows, a funny old horn, and enough modern support to make the bit work.

- **Five ways to find the horn:** Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Gamble, Urza's Saga (its third chapter fetches an artifact costing {0} or {1}, and the horn costs {1}) and Inventors' Fair.
- **The echo pedals:** Panharmonicon and Roaming Throne. With Throne naming Minotaur, our rules reviewer checked that they stack, so one nontoken Minotaur entering makes Sethron trigger **three times**. One cow, three tokens.
- **Encores:** Molten Echoes makes hasty token copies, and Descendants' Fury lets a creature that just hit a player be traded for a creature from the top of your library that shares a type with it.

## Budget Jank: the garage-band version

**Cap: Under $100, basic lands excluded.** This version comes in at **$69.44 excluding basic lands**, at the listed printings (Scryfall USD, 2026-10-29 19:20 JST). That leaves plenty of room for snacks. One small tip: buy the printings on the list. On 2026-10-26, the same cards at Scryfall's default printings came to $102.72, which is over the cap.

**The joke survives completely.** Sethron, Didgeridoo, Deathbellow War Cry (up to four Minotaurs with different names, straight onto the battlefield), Shared Animosity, Rage Reflection, Patriarch's Bidding and the Anaba family are all still here. Budget actually has the *most* creatures of the three versions (31), with a guest list full of character:

- **Hurloon Minotaur**, the vanilla cow from Alpha (1993). It does absolutely nothing, and we love it.
- **Hurloon Shaman**: a 2/3 that reads *"When this creature dies, each player sacrifices a land of their choice."* That includes you. Very bovine of it.
- **Anaba Bodyguard** and **Anaba Shaman** join the family reunion.
- **Tahngarth, Talruum Hero**: taps to deal damage equal to its power to a creature, and that creature hits back. (It's not a fight, if someone at the table asks.)
- **Ox of Agonas**: not technically a Minotaur, just an ox who wandered in. It discards your hand and draws three.
- **Universal Automaton**: a one-mana changeling that counts as a Minotaur for both the horn and Sethron.

**What the garage band gives up, honestly:**

- **Fewer ways to find the horn.** Five premium tutors become two: **Reckless Handling** and **Diabolic Tutor**. Reckless Handling discards a card *at random* after it searches, and that card can be the Didgeridoo you just found. It happens. If an artifact gets discarded, each opponent takes 2 as a consolation prize.
- **No echo pedals.** Without Panharmonicon and Roaming Throne, each Minotaur brings one token, not three.
- **Slower and more exposed.** Several dual lands become tapped lands, and Lightning Greaves becomes Hammer of Purphoros (haste for the whole herd, but no shroud). Artifact removal on the horn hurts most here.
- **Pyre of Heroes** comes in to climb the creature curve one mana value at a time. It works best with nontoken creatures: a Sethron token has mana value 0, so the only card a Sethron token can climb to is Universal Automaton.

## Maximum Jank: the Moo Tube Philharmonic

**Cap: under $1,000.** This version lists at **$552.83 including basic lands** at the listed cheapest printings (Scryfall USD, 2026-10-26).

Let's be clear: this isn't the "best" version. It's the *loudest* one. Maximum Jank asks what happens if the horn becomes a full brass section, and then hands the audience some coins to flip.

**The headliner: Conspiracy.** Name Minotaur, and *your* creatures, *your* creature spells and *your* creature cards outside the battlefield (hand and library included) all become Minotaurs. Noncreature cards don't change, and neither does anything your opponents control. Our rules review checked the punchline: **Didgeridoo can now put any creature card from your hand onto the battlefield**, and Sethron triggers on any nontoken creature you control. Ox of Agonas is a Minotaur now. It didn't ask to be.

**The rest of the orchestra:**

- **Mana Echoes**: whenever a creature enters, you may add {C} equal to the number of creatures you control that share a type with it, counting itself if it's yours (checked). A big herd means a lot of mana for the next horn blast. Spend it fast, because it disappears at the end of the step.
- **Panharmonicon + Roaming Throne** (kept from the original): three Sethron triggers per nontoken Minotaur (checked).
- **Cryptic Gateway**: basically a second horn. Tap two untapped creatures you control to put in a creature card that shares a type with both. Brand-new Sethron tokens can pay that cost, since it isn't a {T} cost (checked).
- **Rings of Brighthearth**: pay {2} to copy a horn blast. *Conditional:* the copy only does something if you still have a second Minotaur card in hand when it resolves.
- **Kindred Charge**: copy every Minotaur you control for a turn. The fine print, which was checked: copies of *other* Minotaurs are tokens, so they don't trigger Sethron. A token copy of Sethron does trigger its own ability, and then the legend rule shows up. The copies *do* trigger Mana Echoes.
- **Coat of Arms**: the herd gets bigger as the herd gets bigger. It's symmetrical, though, so other players' creatures that share types also get the bonus.

**The Ice Age 1995 reunion tour.** Necropotence, Hecatomb, Soul Burn and Game of Chaos all come along. Hecatomb wants four creatures sacrificed when it enters, and four Sethron tokens will do. After that, each untapped Swamp is a 1-damage ping (checked).

**The coin-flip corner.** Krark's Thumb (Mirrodin, 2003) and Karplusan Minotaur (Coldsnap, 2006) team up with Game of Chaos. With Thumb, you flip two coins and ignore one. Karplusan pings when you win a flip and lets an opponent aim the ping when you lose one. Game of Chaos doubles the life stakes with every flip, and it absolutely can go wrong for you. *Conditional:* the idea that only the coin you keep counts as won or lost is implied by the word "ignore," but we haven't found an official ruling that says so.

**Chaos, on purpose:** Warmonger (any player can activate it, and it hits you too), Citanul Flute, Pyre of Heroes, Cavern of Souls, and more Anaba and Hurloon cows.

**What it gives up:** Some of the original's combat support (Descendants' Fury, Vanquisher's Banner), Blasphemous Act, Lightning Greaves and a handful of Minotaurs, in exchange for more ways to blow itself up. Our reviewers note it can stall or self-destruct. We consider that part of the show.

## What a night with the Moo Tube looks like

*A quick heads-up first: this is the kind of game the cards are built to create, based on their text and our rules review. We haven't playtested these lists yet, so treat it as a promise of vibes, not a match report.*

It starts quietly. You play a one-mana artifact, and someone asks what it does. You get to say "it's a didgeridoo," which is half the reason this deck exists.

Then the horn gets blown. For {3}, a Minotaur walks in without being cast, Sethron makes it a friend, and suddenly there's a herd. In the original or Maximum, the echo pedals turn one cow into three tokens, and the table starts counting out loud. Somebody will probably say "moo." Let them.

At some point Deathbellow War Cry brings four different Minotaurs at once, or Patriarch's Bidding brings back the whole barn (and, to be fair, everyone else's favorite creature type). Sethron's pump goes on, menace and haste come with it, and the herd charges.

It won't always work. The horn might get blown up. Reckless Handling might discard the very horn it found. In Maximum, Game of Chaos might double its way into disaster, Warmonger might hit you too, and Coat of Arms might make someone else's army enormous. That's fine. Those are the stories you'll tell at the next game night.

Win or lose, you'll have spent the evening saying "Lowdown Moo Tube" with a straight face. We call that a successful night.
